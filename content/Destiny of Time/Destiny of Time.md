## Chapter 1

In the land of Akatonijia, in the small village of Akaton; up on a grassy knoll by the city wall, there sat three persons. By the way they looked from a distance, one could tell that they were quite young. These three friends were sitting under the large trees in the plain, talking about nothing in particular. Their names were Louis Henderson, Hons Hontogon, And Tweed. Louis, who was also known as Little Lou, was one of us; humans I mean. He had brown-gold hair and blue eyes. Hons had dark brown hair and brown eyes, and was a bit older than Lou. Hons was a being similar to Lou, but not human. He was one known as a royter. Royters are just humans from weird world, the only difference is that they have better senses and can live longer. Tweed, the youngest, was a creature called a Tweedle. He had gold hair and deep black eyes. Hons and Tweed both lived in the Village of Akaton near each other, while Lou lived in the province of Metropolitan. If you have read about this place before, you will know that Lou was the son of Weird World’s president, and was just visiting the village. Louis had only previously been to Weird World, but his father had served as president for many years after discovering the world.

Anyways, back to the story. The three were just talking about Metrok, the city where Lou lived.

 “It’s really fun exploring the city!” Little Lou was just telling Hons and Tweed, “there are night clubs and restaurants and lots of stores and malls.” He said.

 “Do you think we’ll ever get to visit.” Asked Hons.

 “Yeah!” Said Little Lou, “and maybe we can go to a billiard hall together.”

 “What’s a billiard?” asked Tweed and Hons at the same time.

Little Lou was about to answer when the ground started to rumble. A flock of birds flew out of the tree above them.

 “What’s that!?” exclaimed Hons, as he, Tweed and Lou got up.

 “It sounds like an earthquake!” yelled Lou above the roar of the ground shaking.

Suddenly, out of the sky, a large dark object hurled past them, headed north towards the mountains.

 “What!” yelled Tweed, “ was that the moon!?”

a loud crash sounded above them, and Hons looked up to see a moon he had never seen before hurdle towards the north, but this one came from the east. The two collided in the sky above the village, giant in size, although they didn’t seem too big because they were still thousands of miles away. The crash sent debris flying through the atmosphere, nearly hitting them.

 “Quick” yelled Lou to Hons, “let’s run back to your house!”

The three took off running, panting for breath as louder crashes sounded in the heavens above. Long, enormous shadows danced across the grass, formed by giant masses colliding in the sky. None of the three dared to look up and see what though. Villagers could be heard yelling and running for cover from the town as the three ran around the dirt bend and towards Hons' house. They reached the wooden porch, their lungs aching, and ran inside. Loud rumbling could still be heard outside as they shut the door and ran into Hons' kitchen. The pans were heard rumbling in the cupboards and several dishes lay strewn about the floor, cracked. Hons ran upstairs to find his parents while Lou and Tweed wondered aloud to each other what was going on. Suddenly, Hons burst from down the wooden steps leading upstairs back to the group and yelled,

 “I can’t find my parents! Maybe they ran for cover!”

 “Let’s go to my house!” Said Tweed.

The three had just started running towards the door when the rumbling and crashing suddenly ceased, and a dead silence covered the house.

 “Is it over?” wondered Lou aloud.

They opened the door and walked onto the porch. Only low rumbles like fireworks could be heard in the distance. Several villagers were walking around aimlessly. Hons ran up to one he recognized as his neighbor Sarim.

 “Sarim!” Hons called, “have you seen my parents anywhere?”

Sarim looked at Hons with no special recognition, then asked,

 “Did you say something to me boy?”

Hons stopped in his tracks,

 “What do you mean _boy_… uh, it’s me, Hons.”

 “Do I know you?” Sarim asked, partially to Hons and Partially to himself.

Hons was perplexed,

“Sarim, what’s wrong?!”

Sarim simply stared ahead, ignoring Hons' question,

 “It seems like I do know you.” He said, “but like, from some sort of dream.”

Hons couldn’t understand. Sarim just turned away and kept walking aimlessly around. Hons walked back to the others.

 “What’s wrong?” asked Lou.

 “And why doesn’t he recognize you?” asked Tweed.

Hons replied, “I don’t know, it seems like all of the villagers are the same, though.”

 “Let’s go to see if my parents are home.” Suggested Tweed again.

 “I have to go back to see if dad is all right.” Said Lou. He took off running to the steamship port.

 “Well,” said Hons to Tweed, “let’s go to your house then.”

As they turned to go, they heard an unknown, high-pitched voice behind them,

 “Hey! You!”

Hons and Tweed spun around and looked.

 “Who said that?” asked Hons, startled.

 “There’s no one here but us!” added Tweed.

 “Down here!” said the voice.

Hons and Tweed looked down. A small, gray Fuzzbuzz stood at their feet, with a white patch of fur running up its stomach to its face. It wore a purple robe draped over the top half of its body, and a matching, purple wizard’s hood. He held a small, long staff in his right paw with a strange emblem on the end.

 “I’m sorry, but you won’t find your Parents there” he said, motioning to Tweed’s house with his staff.

 “Who are you?” asked Tweed.

 “How do you know we were looking for his parents.” Added Hons.

The Fuzzbuzz twitched its whiskers and replied, “I am Kubo, Wizard of Time.” he continued, “also, I know you are Hons, and he is Tweed.”

Tweed gasped,

“How do you know our names!?”

Kubo replied,

 “I am the oracle of time, and have possessed this power since my creation. I also know what happened to cause this great calamity.”

 “What happened?” asked Hons.

Kubo answered,

 “That I will gladly reveal to you, but now is not the time and here is not the place. Listen, you must trust me. We must go somewhere first and then I will explain all.”

 “Where?” asked Hons.

 “The shrine of shrines at the north.” Replied Kubo, “that is where we need to go to restore the world.”

 “The ancient chapel past the village square?” asked Hons, “ it’s guarded day and night by guards, and some of the villagers even say that it’s haunted.”

Kubo lifted one ear and replied,

 “It was once said, “legends are made by those who would rather not know the truth.” I assure you, it is not haunted at all, but enchanted with sacred powers of the forces.”

 “I still don’t know how we can get inside without breaking in.” said Tweed flatly.

Kubo smiled,

 “Well, one thing I forgot to mention, is that I have indeed dabbled in the white magic of my fellow ancients and can get us there even now.”

At this, Hons and Tweed looked at each other, wide-eyed.

 “Are you ready?” asked Kubo.

 “Well, yeah.” Answered Hons, “what about you, Tweed.”

 “Yeah, I’m ready,” said Tweed.

 “Then let’s go!” exclaimed Kubo, and he turned and ran on all fours toward the north side of the village. Hons and Tweed followed, wondering what would happen to them all.

In less than an hour and a half, the three arrived at the old, stucco building at the north side of the village. Just as Hons had said, guards walked around the shrine, looking this way and that.

 “Even if the guards are entranced like Sarim,” said Hons, “they might still guard the building.”

 “I’m sure they would,” replied Kubo, “that is if I didn’t do this.”

With that, he sheathed his staff to his back and held out his right paw to the door of the chapel. A soft, purple glow, almost like a transparent carpet, spread across the walkway to the shrine and up the steps, all the way to the heavy wooden doors, which then opened as if by…  well, by magic.

 “Come on.” Ordered Kubo as he ran up the walk towards the steps.

Hons and Tweed followed quickly, trying to stay out of eyesight of the guards. Suddenly, one turned and looked right at them.

 “Oh no!” said Tweed.

 “Quick duck!” returned Hons.

Kubo looked back and said,

 “It’s all right! The magic way I have provided allows us to become invisible in their eyes, as long as we stay on the purple path.”

Hons looked from Kubo to the guard, who turned away from them without seeing them at all. They scurried up the steps and joined Kubo, then entered the building. The whole interior reeked of a musty odor of ages past, years of millennia. Hons and Tweed felt a divine presence the moment they stepped inside. The doorway led into a small room which then branched off to the left into another room. They entered the door to the left and halted abruptly. A long, wide hall stretched before them, with rows of old wooden seats lining the sides, and a long carpet stretched to the front of the hall, where a dark, wood podium sat, a strange object of glass and smooth stone on the top. Kubo walked into the center of the hall, turned to them and said,

 “This is the shrine of shrines.”

Chapter 2

As Hons and Tweed looked around in wonder, Kubo started up the steps to the podium and climbed onto it.

 “Now,” he said, “ I will explain to you the legend of time. You might want to sit on one of those chairs, because it might take a while.”

Once Hons and Tweed were seated, Kubo began his story,

 “ long long ago, when this chapel was first built, I set up in it six mirrored elements of the worlds to protect the flow of time.”

 “You were there?!” asked Tweed, puzzled.

 “Yes” replied Kubo, “ I am the great wizard and have existed since the dawn of time. I built this shrine along with other ancients to protect the six elements and this,”

He motioned to the glass structure on the podium, which was actually a small, round bottle, covered with a stone bust of a Fuzzbuzz.

 “This is the sacred water of time” explained Kubo, “ it was given to us ancients by one of the Great Deity’s chief gods, Askalon; and rumor has it, that it comes from the great fountain of life in the heavens. But no one knows for sure. Anyways, this water is used to heal and, more importantly, to purify the ancient weapon of time.”

Kubo motioned to the back of the room where pillars supported the roof, and long, purple curtains covered in intricate patterns and symbols lined the entrance to another room.

 “That is the room where the sacred sword of time is held.” Said Kubo, “but we may only use it in a time of great need, even greater than now, when we are faced with the true evil that started this disaster.”

He paused,

“One day, when all of this is over, if the Great Deity wills, I will owe you the whole story behind the ancients and the sacred power of Time.

And for some reason, my powers of being an oracle were hindered from knowing. His plan was to overthrow the worlds by mixing up the times and colliding the moons and the two planets. This one and the one called Earth. When the disaster struck, I was awakened from my slumber in this shrine, and rushed down here to see if anything had happened. When I got here, all of the elements are gone.”

He gestured with one paw to a smaller podium below the one he was on. It was also wood, but the top was made of some ancient mineral, much like stone, but not quite. In this were carved six hollows, three on each side and connected with grooves. Also, strange letters in some other language adorned the top.

So, when time was disrupted today, they all returned to the places where I had taken them from. Like  I said, my powers of seeing through time have been hindered by some evil force looming over all of this, but I can feel that simply returning the elements won’t be enough. I can almost feel that you will need to conquer some evil force guarding each element before retrieving it.”

It is in this province, but not here on the mainland. The element you will first need is in the gulf of Analan, on one of the Teclan Islands. It is a stone bust very similar to the one on this podium, and also of a Fuzzbuzz like myself, but it looks a little different.”

 “I would say by steamship, but since this is an emergency, I will use my magic powers to warp you to the island through a time portal.”

 “I would,” replied Kubo, “but I must stay and guard this water and the sacred weapon. Plus, I must try and regain some of my powers so I can tell Louis how to help.”

 “When do we start?” asked Tweed.

 “ As soon as we can” replied Kubo, “are you ready.”

 “Yes!” replied Hons and Tweed as one.

As he said this, Kubo raised both paws and shut his eyes. Then, he recited a spell which Hons and Tweed instantly felt as being very old. “portal to the islands far, come forth now and become ajar.”

At this, a purple, swirling circle appeared in front of Tweed and Hons. Kubo gave them some last advice.

 “when you get to the island, try and find some weapon to fight the evil one I suspect you’ll encounter.  It seems like there is a sword on that island that is hidden somewhere. It is an ancient sword that has been hidden for hundreds of years. Also, the portal should take you to the correct island. But if not, build a skiff to make your way to the other. One last thing: if I figure out anything, I will try to send you a message somehow. All right, whenever you are ready.”

Hons and Tweed said farewell to Kubo, and then, stepped into the portal.

Salty, sea breeze whipped around the two, sea birds called in the distance and overhead. Hons And Tweed opened their eyes, (apparently, they had closed them in the portal) and looked around. They were standing on a sandy beach with charcoal colored sand, and dark, blue water waving up the shore. Hons turned around and noticed that behind them stood a sheer, sandstone-colored rock cliff, maybe 60 feet tall. He and Tweed looked around. A fisherman in a small rowboat was cruising a few yards from the shore. He didn’t appear to be in a trance like the other villagers back in Akaton though. Hons ran up to the surf and called out through cupped hands,

 “ Hey! You sir.”

The man in the boat looked up from his pole and at the shore. Hons waved so he could see him.

 “Ho there!” replied the fisherman, “what can I help ya with?”

Hons cupped his hands again and yelled back, “ Do you know what island this is?”

 “What?” replied the fisherman, “ do you really not know the name of the land you are on? Well, you be on the Teclan islands. The one you’re on is called Tajoon, and the north side specifically.”

 “Thanks.” Yelled Hons.

 “Which island do you think the element is on?” asked Tweed.

Hons answered, “I’m not sure. I know there are three islands, and if I remember correctly, this one and the one south of us called Tit are the most uninhabited. The other is owned by some low-class governor.”

 “We would expect the hidden sword and the element to be on the most uninhabited one, right?” asked Tweed.

 “Just what I thought.” Said Hons, “let’s try and get up on that cliff, maybe we’ll see something.”

He and Tweed walked east along the coast until they came to a spot where the cliff face was sort of crumbled, and looked easy to climb.

 “over there.” Said Hons.

They walked up to the crumbled spot and started to climb. It was easy at first, for the cliff here was not as steep and was only about as high as Hons' house, ~~which had two floors,~~ but gradually it got harder, and Hons and Tweed were forced on their knees to scramble up the steep cliff face. At one point when they were exhausted and about to give up to find another place to climb up, Tweed spied something manmade carved into the rock up ahead.

 “Hons! Look.” he cried.

Hons looked in the direction Tweed was pointing and saw what looked like old, carved stairs in the sandstone rock.

 “ Hey,” he said, “let’s just try to get up there and see what that is.”

For the next few minutes, He and Tweed  struggled up the sharp stone towards the stairs, until finally, Hons reached the top and pulled Tweed up.

 “they are stairs.” Said Hons.

 “and they lead around that round peak ahead.” Added Tweed.

They made their way around to the other side of the large rock and saw what looked like a large boulder that had been rolled over a cave.

  “ How do we move it?” asked Tweed.

Hons looked at the rock, then up the path.

 “Let’s keep walking and see if we find something like an old tree to leverage it out of the cave.” He suggested.

They continued up the path until they found an old, skinny tree growing sideways out of the mountainside.

 “Help me pull it out.” Said Hons to Tweed, and they both started to heave at the dead log.

Hons had an idea. Unsheathing his dirk that he carried with him, he started hacking at the roots of the tree. And with the butt of the knife, banged at the lose sandstone rocks holding them to the cliff. All of a sudden, with a sickening “crunch!”, the tree gave way, and Hons and Tweed pulled it out.

 “great!” said Tweed, “let’s take it back to the cave.”

And they headed back down the path.

Once they reached the boulder, the two shoved the smallest end of the tree under the boulder and began to push and heave on the other end with all of their might.

  “push!” said Hons as he and Tweed gradually lifted the boulder from the mouth of the cave. Finally, the boulder gave way and the two jumped out of the way quickly to avoid being crushed by the giant rock, which tumbled down the cliff face with loud bangs until it plummeted into the sea bellow. Once the dust cleared from the maw of the cave, Hons and Tweed gazed inside, only to realize that it wasn’t a cave at all, just a hollow in the rock. But what was hidden inside that hollow did surprise them.

 Meanwhile, across the ocean in the province of Metropolitan, Little Lou had just landed on the steamship. It had taken him a good part of the day to get there, but he had tried to board the quickest ship listed. He left the ship and ran until he reached the inner city, where he hired a taxi to take him to the capital of Metrok where his father lived. The taxi driver seemed panicked from the disaster, but since he knew Louis’ father, he got him to the capital right away.

Little Lou arrived there in a few minutes and burst into his father’s office.

Thompson Henderson, a tall man with brown eyes and dark hair dressed in a black suit and top hat, pressed to perfection, signaled for his son to sit down at the other end of his desk.

 “I’ve heard Louis,” said his father, “ right now I’m just trying to calm down all of the people, some of them are even entranced somehow. I don’t understand.”

Louis went on to explain to his father everything that had happened since that morning. He also recounted that Hons' neighbor Sarim also seemed in some sort of a dreamy state.

 “ Can I help in any way?” asked Louis when he had finished.

 “Well,” replied Thompson, “ you can go and try to help your mother calm down the citizens down by City Hall Park.”

Louis walked out of the building and headed towards the park, when suddenly, he heard a strange whirring sound. A round, purple portal of some sort suddenly appeared in front of him, about the size of a bathroom mirror. Then, a face of a Fuzzbuzz wearing a purple cloak and hood appeared in it.

The Timemaster 3000 was one of Little Lou’s inventions that one could use for drilling into the earth. There is no time to describe it now. It will be told later. But for now, just know that Little Lou was an inventor, “and a bit of a nerd,” in his spare time.

# Chapter 3

Hons and Tweed gazed at what they had revealed in the cave. A long, dark sword was stuck into a round, stone pedestal, adorned with strange symbols and letters.

 “ This must be the ancient sword that Kubo told us about.” Said Hons.

He walked over to the pedestal and retrieved the sword. He and tweed looked at it. It had a long, slender blade, which was so sharply tapered at the end, that one almost couldn’t tell where the blade ended. At the bottom was an armguard above the hilt in the shape of two wings, and a sinister looking ghoul face between them.

Hons looked out of the mouth of the hollow.

 “ Hey Tweed,” he said, “ it looks like the sun is setting, maybe we should try to find more wood to make a fire.”

He and Tweed walked out of the cave and continued up the path a little farther, Hons still carrying the sword. After a few ways up the trail, they found another stump sticking out of the side of the hill. After stripping several pieces of wood from the stump, Hons and Tweed headed back towards the cave. once they were back at the cave, Hons wondered aloud,

 “ how will we light the fire?”

Tweed looked around and spotted something strange on the ceiling of the hollow.

 “ what’s that weird black rock?” he asked.

Hons looked where tweed was pointing, then exclaimed,

 “ Tweed! That’s not black rock, that’s flint!”

Hons got stood up from arranging the sticks and wood and unsheathed his dirk. He chiseled out some of the black mineral on the cave ceiling, then knelt back down and tried to rub the pieces together. after a few minutes, Hons saw orange sparks leap from the flint, and he quickly held it near the wood.

 “Tweed,” he said, “ can you stand over there by the cave entrance to block the wind?”

Tweed obeyed and Hons continued to rub the stones together, blowing softly on them.

After a while, he finally managed to start a small flame, which eventually turned into a small fire. Hons got up and headed outside,

 “ I’ll be right back.” He told Tweed.

Hons returned several minutes later with a handful of small acorns he had found on a briar up the trail. He and Tweed sat on the cave floor and began trying to open the small seeds. Hons first tried placing the nuts on the cave floor and slicing them open with his dirk, but it didn’t work. Next, Tweed suggested that they hit the seeds with the butt of the knife, but that only resulted in the acorn breaking on the cave floor and the seeds inside becoming powder. Finally, Hons had an idea. He grabbed the sword from behind them and, using the long, tapered end of the blade, forced it between the two sections of the acorn and pried it open. He and Tweed ate the few seeds inside, then did the same with the rest. When they were finally content with their meal, Hons said,

 “ we should spend the night here, and then look for the element tomorrow.”

 “all right.” Agreed Tweed.

They took off their top tunics and lay them on the cave floor to serve as patting to sleep on. Before Hons went to bed though, he got up and untied his headband. Unsheathing his knife, he wrapped the headband around the hilt, and then, held the blade over the fire for several minutes. After a while, he took the knife, still wrapped in his headband, and laid it at the mouth of the hollow.

 “ that should keep out any unwanted company.” He said to Tweed.

Then, as the embers of the fire went out, and with the sound of the crashing sea below them, Hons and Tweed lay down on the cave floor, and drifted off to sleep.

Hons awoke early the next morning. He sat up and rubbed his eyes, then strapped his headband back to his forehead. He looked out of the hollow towards the north. The surf crashed far below, seagulls called across the blue expanse of water, and a faint glow from the rising sun in the east signaled the start of a new day. Hons reached over and shook Tweed,

 “ Tweed, hey Tweed!” he said, “ we need to go find the element now. Get up!”

Tweed groaned, then sat up, trying to flatten down his hair.

 “ We should get going right away.” Said Hons, then walked over and picked up his dirk from the cave entrance, which had cooled during the night. Tweed got up and grabbed the sword they had found the day before, and handed it to Hons. He took it and then motioned for Tweed to follow him out of the cave. After putting on their tunics and buckling their boots, the two set out, higher up the path. They started following it west for a few ways, then it twisted around in a loop until they faced south, and then east. By now they had circled around to the top of the boulder with the hollow. Walking east, they headed higher up the mountain until they came to a bend in the path. When they turned the corner, Hons and Tweed stopped abruptly. Just ahead, there was a complete drop off to the rocky crags below, where the sea could be heard breaking on the sharp rocks far below. The only way across the cliff was an old, Rickety bridge that spanned the length of the gap, probably fifty or sixty feet across. Hons turned to Tweed and suggested,

 “ We can cross it single file; I’ll go in front, and you take the back. OK?”

 “ All right.” Agreed Tweed. 

Hons placed one foot on the bridge and tested it carefully. It seemed to be sturdy, although who knows how old the thing was. Hons and Tweed steadily made their way across the bridge, trying not to glance over the side at the sharp rocks and pounding sea far below. Soon they reached the other side, and stepped quickly onto solid ground.

Hons was about to tell Tweed how frightening the experience had been, when suddenly, they heard a loud rustling in a cluster of scrub brush near the mountainside. Two large wolf-like creatures leapt out of the bushes and ran strait toward Hons and Tweed. These beasts, (which are known as Woufuls) had the similar body of a wolf, but with an extra pair of hands stretching up from their torsos, and at the end of their long bushy tails, had a sharp clad with iron spikes, almost like a mace. The two beasts howled at Hons and Tweed, who cried out in fear, but Hons took quick action,

 “ Tweed! Hurry, take my dirk,” he said, tossing the knife to Tweed.

Then, he grabbed the ancient sword with both hands and charged at one of the beasts. It leapt up on him, but Hons ducked quickly and, just as the creature was about to land on him, stuck up the sword and pierced its stomach. The Woufuls howled in pain, and fell in a heap on the dirt next to Hons. At the same time, Tweed had caught the attention of the other animal, and had jumped out of the way just as it leapt at him. he quickly took a slash at the beast’s upper arm, injuring it. The wolf howled and turned to Tweed. Just as it leapt up to attack him though, Hons jumped up with the sword and slammed it into the beast’s side, sending it sprawling across the dirt. The wolf slid across the path and over the edge of the cliff, down to its death to the sharp rocks below.

Hons and Tweed stood panting for breath after the attack was over, then Tweed asked,

 “ Do you think that was the evil power Kubo spoke of?”

Hons caught his breath and replied,

 “ I don’t know, but let’s keep a sharp lookout on the way up, just in case.”

He stood up and rubbed the sword against the mountainside to clean the blade. Then said,

 “ Come on, you can keep my dirk just in case we meet any other wild animals. Let’s keep walking up towards the top.

 “ All right, let’s go.” Agreed Tweed.

The two stood up and continued up the mountain towards the top. They had no more interruptions from any other creatures on the way.

Dim light from the street lamps illuminated the dark sidewalk. A Long, metal building stretched the length of the block, large windows adorned the upper half of it. The only noise heard was the crickets chirping and a low buzzing noise coming from the air conditioning unit of the building. Suddenly, the stillness of the night was broken by a strange humming noise, and a round, purple swirling circle appeared in the small lot on the side of the building. Out of the portal stepped Little Lou. He turned and talked to someone in the portal for a minute, then turned and ran towards the dark building. As he made his way to the door, the portal behind him disappeared as quickly as it had appeared. Louis ran up to the door and opened the hatch to a small, metal box to the left. It was a security access code panel. He typed in a few digits, then heard the familiar click of the door being unlocked. Louis cautiously opened the door and walked in to the right, where he flicked a power switch on the wall. Almost immediately, large bar lights overhead hummed to life. Louis ran down the length of the building until he reached a certain spot and turned right. There was a small room to the left. And along the floor lay a strange object covered with a tarp. Iron rails from under the thing ran across the floor for a way before disappearing into a circular tunnel-arch of sorts. Little Lou ran over into the small control room and pressed a few buttons, pulled a switch, and then activated some sort of program.
